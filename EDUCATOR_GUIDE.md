# Educator Guide — Meadow Balance Lab

## Audience

Designed for learners around age 9.

Suggested session length: 10–15 minutes, with optional extra exploration.

## Prerequisites

Learners benefit from knowing that:

- plants are living organisms;
- animals need food;
- sunlight and water help plants grow.

No prior ecosystem vocabulary is required.

## Learning objectives

Learners should be able to:

1. identify producers and consumers;
2. recognize herbivores and predators;
3. explain that plants depend on environmental resources;
4. predict a simple effect of changing water or sunlight;
5. explain why an animal without suitable food cannot thrive indefinitely;
6. distinguish a simplified simulation from a real ecosystem.

## Activity 1 — Build

Ask the learner:

- Which organisms are producers?
- Which animals eat plants?
- Which animal eats another animal?

The learner builds a meadow containing:

- at least two producers;
- at least two herbivores;
- at least one predator.

The application gives immediate feedback and allows unlimited retries.

## Activity 2 — Predict and experiment

Begin with the healthy example.

Ask:

What do you think will happen to the plants if water becomes scarce?

Then:

1. choose a scenario;
2. record a prediction;
3. advance the model one step;
4. compare the prediction with the result;
5. read what changed and why.

Try more than one starting scenario.

Remind learners that all displayed values are model values, not real animal or plant measurements.

## Activity 3 — Restore balance

Open the unfamiliar struggling meadow.

Ask:

- What clues show that the habitat is struggling?
- Which resource may be limiting plant growth?
- What change would you test first?

Allow the learner to adjust conditions and retry.

## Model assumptions

The model is deliberately simplified and deterministic.

- Values are bounded from 0 to 100.
- Plants depend on sunlight and water.
- The more limiting resource constrains modeled plant growth.
- Herbivores depend on modeled plant food.
- Predators depend on modeled prey.
- Identical inputs and actions produce identical results.
- Negative, infinite, and NaN population values are prevented.

These are illustrative classroom model units, not measurements.

## Scientific limitations

Real ecosystems are much more complex.

The model does not represent every factor, including:

- seasons;
- migration;
- reproduction rates;
- disease;
- decomposition;
- soil chemistry;
- temperature;
- competition;
- biodiversity;
- extreme weather;
- human intervention.

Use the wording:

"What does this simplified model predict?"

Avoid presenting the result as an exact prediction of nature.

## Differentiation

For learners needing support:

- read definitions aloud;
- use the hint buttons;
- focus first on producer-to-herbivore relationships;
- compare only two conditions at a time;
- discuss a prediction before selecting it.

For learners ready for extension:

- identify factors missing from the model;
- discuss why real ecosystems react differently;
- add decomposers to a paper diagram;
- compare a food chain with a food web.

## Adult and learner co-play

An adult can ask for reasoning before each simulation step.

Suggested pattern:

1. learner makes a prediction;
2. adult asks why;
3. learner advances the model;
4. both compare prediction and result.

Incorrect predictions should be treated as opportunities to investigate, not as failure.

## Offline follow-up

Draw an ecosystem on paper containing:

- two producers;
- two consumers;
- one predator.

Draw arrows showing energy flow from food to consumer.

Then ask:

What might change if water became scarce?

## Accessibility

The activities do not require dragging.

Core controls can be used with a keyboard.

Textual labels accompany visual indicators so meaning does not depend only on color.

Reduced-motion preferences are supported.

## Privacy

The application collects no learner information.

No name, email, login, analytics, advertising, payment, chat, or upload is used.

Progress remains only in the current page session.

Using Reset lesson or reloading the page clears learner state.
